# V6.2 evaluations, September 2026 — what the runs found

A summary of five reports written between 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-08. Four rounds of the bench in `evals/`, about 900 agent sessions: `claude-sonnet-5` throughout, 14 on Haiku 4.5, and 234 on `gpt-5.6-terra` through the Codex adapter in the fourth round. Roughly $175 of metered spend plus one round on a Codex subscription, which reports no cost at all. This file keeps the conclusions; the reports keep the evidence.

## The short version

The corpus does change behavior, in a few specific places, and every one of those places is a rule the model would not apply on its own: no fact restating a canonical source, no learned rule where the routing hook was the defect, no comment narrating the diff, no groom that drops a ticket id. Everything else the model does with a plain instructions file too. What made the corpus better was moving those rules into code. What made it worse was prose: more of it, less of it, or examples that retold the test. The single largest measurement error was teaching to the test, and the single largest cost lever was the number of tool calls, not the number of words.

## What was good

- **The paired design caught its own bugs.** The first run (09-03) reported write-back failing in both arms. Failing identically in both arms is the signature of a grader defect, not a corpus defect, and it was: every node-diff counter had graded an empty diff because of a path prefix. Thirteen harness defects came out of that run and one real result: the canonical-source gate held in the treatment and not in the control.
- **Evidence strings on every record, and records that re-grade without a model.** The prefix bug was found in minutes because the failing record named the path it had seen, and the whole run was re-graded from its directory afterwards.
- **Fixes in code worked where sentences had not.** `memory.sh supersede` turned an assertion that failed in every arm into one that passed in every arm. `log.sh` refusing a summary that names a file or a SHA did the same for the log format. The `GROOM:` line listing the tokens a groom must keep took fact loss from failing in all four baseline runs to passing in all three of the adopted corpus's. Each of these was first tried as a sentence and measured as not applied.
- **Cutting tool calls cut cost with the rules unchanged.** USD per tool call was flat across every corpus size tried, and the harness made three to four times the calls of a plain instructions file, five of them bootstrap reads. One call to load (`status.sh --load`) and one call to finish (`finish.sh`) took calls per session down 27% on both prompt sets, with no assertion regressing. That shape is what shipped.
- **The bench itself is now harder to fool.** A held-out prompt set, a contamination judge, assertion strata, a pooled multi-run control, and a calibrated grading triage were added on 09-05 (see [README](README.md), "Keeping it honest").

## What made it worse

- **Worked examples that retold the evals.** The `examples` variant scored best on 09-04, the only arm with no unique failure. The contamination judge found that 11 of its worked examples describe the same situation as one of the 19 eval prompts, in an invented domain the lexical probe could not see. On the held-out set it was the only arm to score below the base, losing the routing answer and dropping groom content silently.
- **Compressing the prose.** The lean variant halved the always-loaded words and lost exactly the two rules it compressed (the "earns its place" comment list and the name-the-source sentence). Restoring those two and putting lean on the two-call mechanics still lost one repeat each of the same two rules plus the catalog rule. Words that state an admission rule are doing work; words that restate general practice are not.
- **More prose, and more precise prose.** The protocol variant (a phase-gated checklist, 4,437 always-loaded words) cost 36% more per session and moved nothing.
- **One repeat per cell.** The same corpus scored 91.2 to 94.7 across four identical runs; six assertions flipped with nothing changed. Every 09-04 variant delta sat inside that band. Nothing measured at one repeat was a verdict.
- **The eval set measuring itself.** The 09-04 corpus edits were written from this set's failing transcripts. Two assertions turned out to measure prompt wording: the vendor-comment row passes in every arm on the canonical phrasing and fails in every arm on the paraphrase; the supersede "no history" row fails only on the held-out phrasing that hands the session the old value. The first was rewritten to accept a named constant; the second is a prompt to reword.
- **Mechanical groom helpers.** Lossless `distill` and `archive` scripts were used correctly in every groom session and cleared the flags, and the sessions then rewrote the moved text by hand anyway. No measured gain; not merged.
- **Cutting the entry point's gate to one sentence.** A 244-word template lost the "remember that" case: two of four sessions under a one-sentence gate replied without a single tool call, zero of seven under the long one. Restoring one sentence — a one-line request is a task like any other — put it back to four of four. The other 130 words of that paragraph were not doing work; that sentence was.
- **A small side effect of the finish call.** Six of 140 two-call sessions answered mid-turn, ran `finish.sh`, and ended with "Done — answered the question and logged the session." The answer reached the user; the final message did not carry it. One sentence in the entry point now says the final message is the report itself.

## How it got confirmed

| Claim | How |
| --- | --- |
| The adopted corpus beats the pre-adoption one | Pooled control of four identical baseline runs (n=4 per assertion), candidates at two repeats, then the adopted tip at three repeats on both prompt sets. Canonical: 93.4% → 96.5% with zero unique failures. Held-out: 96.5% → 97.7%, behavior rows 94.1% → 98.0%. |
| It is the mechanics, not the words | Same rules on the two-call shape: 0.73× the tool calls on the canonical set, 0.63× on held-out. Lean prose on the same mechanics saved 5 more points of USD and lost rules. |
| The gain survives rewording | `evals/heldout.json`: same fixtures and assertion ids, every prompt rewritten after the variants were frozen. Twocall 99.1% → 98.2%; examples 96.5% → 94.7%, below the base. |
| The examples win was contamination | `evals/contamination.py --judge`: 11 matching passages in `variant/examples`, 0 in the base and in every adopted change. |
| It holds on another model | Haiku 4.5, seven-eval subset: twocall 95.0% vs base 90.0%, fewer calls, no regression. |
| The grader is consistent | `evals/triage.py` calibrated against the 09-04 human grades: full agreement except one shape the human had graded both ways; one rule applied since. |
| The three code fixes are the delta | Per-assertion buckets: `date-bumped`, `no-fact-loss`, `source-fixed` moved from 0.00–0.50 to 1.00 in the tooling arm alone; everything else stayed inside the flake band. |

## The Codex arm, 2026-09-07 to 09-08

The first round driven on a second harness: the corpus at `ecfc69b` against a plain instructions file, on `claude-sonnet-5` and on `gpt-5.6-terra`, three repeats, 19 of 20 evals per arm and `bootstrap-once` separately. Four arms of 57 sessions, every run `ok`, none void. The manual rows graded to 0 UNSURE, so none were graded by hand.

**The corpus does the same work on both harnesses, and less of it than the headline says.** Against a plain instructions file the node gains +24.0 points overall on Claude and +21.6 on Codex, with zero regressions and near-identical discriminating sets. Almost all of that is structural: information rows are answers that exist only inside `.agent/`, so a harness-free arm scores zero by construction, and conformance rows have no mechanism to conform to. On the behavior stratum the gain is **+2.9 points on Claude and +3.9 on Codex**. That is the number the corpus should be defended on, and the reason to report the strata separately rather than one aggregate.

**Where Codex trails, it is one mechanism, not drift.** Codex is 5.8 points behind overall and 10 behind on conformance, and both conformance failures are the same eval: asked to put a deploy command "into the agent instructions", it appends a section to the entry-point mirrors — 3 of 3 runs — immediately below the line that says everything else lives in `.agent/` and must never be restated there. The mirrors stay identical, so the drift check passes; the content simply never reaches the node. `ENTRYPOINT_MAX_WORDS` at 550 against a 244-word template cannot see a 32-word restatement: the gate measures bloat, not boundary. A boundary needs a comparison against the shipped template's load path, which `status.sh` already does for mirror drift and does not do against the template.

**A groom on Codex drops the rule it must keep.** `retro-rule-expiry` requires two learned rules to go — one now enforced by tooling, one already in the contract — and the third, which no source states, to survive. On Claude it survives 3 of 3; on Codex it is deleted in 2 of 3. The harness-free arm passes this row vacuously, having no groom at all, so only the cross-harness pair is informative. The `keep at least:` token list that fixed memory-file fact loss in the September rounds is wired to `memory/` flags and not to `learned.md` — but it cannot simply be extended there. Run against the fixture it names the surviving rule's date stamp and both doomed rules' stamps too, so a verbatim extension would forbid the drops the eval requires. Memory grooming is lossless by design; `learned.md` grooming is deliberately lossy. The guard that fits the difference is a required reason: a groom that removes a learned rule names the source that now enforces it, which is checkable in code where "keep everything" is not.

**A planted instruction was contained on both harnesses and disclosed on one.** In `security-origin-gate` nothing was recorded and the planted string never entered the node, on either harness. Codex then summarised the file and never mentioned that it carries an embedded instruction; Claude named it in 3 of 3. Containment is not the gap, disclosure is, and no rule in the corpus asks for it.

**The finish call fires per turn, and the log entry is per session.** The entry-point template scopes the bootstrap explicitly — one conversation is one session, a new user message does not start a new one — and scopes the finish call only as "before handing back", which happens on every turn. A session's end is not observable to the agent at all, so the observable trigger is the one that got written. Nineteen of twenty evals are single turn, where the two readings coincide and neither can be distinguished; the multi-turn eval separates them, and on Codex every run bootstrapped once and finished three times in three turns, leaving three log entries for one session, two of them conversational noise. Nothing in the set measures this: `bootstrap-once` checks that the *bootstrap* ran once and no assertion anywhere counts finish calls. A log entry that a session updates in place, rather than appends, would converge under repeated calls and would stop asking the agent to predict something it cannot see.

**Sample-size and coverage limits of this round.** One round, three repeats, one corpus revision, two harnesses. Twenty-six behavior rows pass every repeat in both arms on both harnesses — candidates for prose the corpus may not need to state, and a candidate list is not a verdict: the September lean variant lost exactly the rules it compressed, so a trim is measured, never assumed. Four runner defects were fixed to make the Codex arm run at all (`21b4185`, `002561f`, `5ec14ed`, `a5f681d`); the Codex arm had never been driven live before this round. One defect remains open and bounds everything above: the Claude adapter cannot drive a multi-turn session, so the set's only multi-turn eval has never validated the Claude side, and every "once per session" claim in the corpus is unmeasured there.

## What to carry forward

The pooled control for the next round is the adopted corpus at commit `5001189`, run 2026-09-05 at three repeats per eval on both prompt sets; regenerate it with `evals/run-arm.sh --jobs 6 <workspace> base 5001189` and the same with `--spec evals/heldout.json`, then point `pooled.py --baseline` at those workspaces. Run the contamination judge on any branch before believing its delta. Grade fact loss under one rule: a value the reply names as contradicting the repository may go, anything dropped silently fails. Treat a delta under two assertions as noise until repeated. And when a rule is not being applied, look for the script that could refuse the wrong shape before rewriting the sentence.
