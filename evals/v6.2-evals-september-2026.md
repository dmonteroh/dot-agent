# V6.2 evaluations, September 2026

Four rounds of the bench in `evals/`, run between 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-08. About 900 agent sessions. `claude-sonnet-5` throughout, 14 sessions on Haiku 4.5, and 234 on `gpt-5.6-terra` through the Codex adapter in the fourth round. Roughly $175 of metered spend, plus one round on a Codex subscription that reports no cost at all.

Every percentage below is an assertion pass rate over the 20-eval set. The five reports in `tmp/` hold the evidence. This file holds the numbers and what follows from them.

## Does the node change what an agent does?

Round four put the node against a plain 224-word instructions file, on two harnesses, three repeats, 19 of 20 evals per arm.

| Arm | overall | behavior | conformance | information |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Claude, plain instructions file | 72.5 | 91.2 | 51.7 | 0 |
| Claude, node | 96.5 | 94.1 | 100.0 | 100 |
| Codex, plain instructions file | 69.0 | 86.3 | 50.0 | 0 |
| Codex, node | 90.6 | 90.2 | 90.0 | 100 |

No assertion regressed against the control on either harness.

Read the behavior column, not the overall column. Information assertions ask for answers that exist only inside `.agent/`, so an arm without it scores zero by construction. Conformance assertions ask for a format or a script that an arm without the node cannot produce. Both strata are near-vacuous in the control, and together they carry most of the +24.0 and +21.6 headline gains.

**The node's measured contribution to what a model does on its own is +2.9 points on Claude and +3.9 on Codex.** That is the number to defend. It is small, positive, and consistent across two harnesses.

## Did the V6.2 changes beat what the field was running?

Round three compared the adopted corpus (`5001189`) against the pre-adoption revision the field was running. Three repeats, against a four-run pooled control.

| Prompt set | Corpus | overall | behavior | conformance | information | unique fails |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| canonical | pre-adoption | 93.4 | 93.4 | 93.8 | 91.7 | – |
| canonical | adopted | 96.5 | 95.1 | 100.0 | 88.9 | 0 |
| held-out | pre-adoption | 96.5 | 94.1 | 100.0 | 100.0 | – |
| held-out | adopted | 97.7 | 98.0 | 96.7 | 100.0 | 1 |

The held-out set is the same twenty situations with every prompt reworded, frozen before the variants ran. The gain survives the rewording, which is the check that separates a real rule from a corpus tuned to a phrasing. The adopted shape scored 99.1 on the canonical set and 98.2 on the paraphrase, measured as a variant before adoption.

It also survives a change of model. On a seven-eval subset run against Haiku 4.5, the adopted shape scored 95.0 against the base's 90.0, with fewer calls and no regression.

## What it cost

The adopted corpus runs at 0.73× the tool calls of the pre-adoption one on the canonical set, and 0.63× on held-out. USD per tool call was flat across every corpus size measured, so calls are what a session costs, not words. One call to bootstrap (`status.sh --load`) and one to finish (`finish.sh`) took calls per session down 27% with no assertion regressing. That shape shipped.

Codex against Claude on the same node: 0.79× the tool calls, 1.83× the wall time. There is no USD figure for the Codex arm, because the subscription stream reports no cost.

## What moved the numbers

Code, not sentences. Three assertions moved in the tooling arm alone, against the four-run pooled control.

| Assertion | base | tooling arm | What changed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| `continuity-supersede/date-bumped` | 0.00 | 1.00 | The preset names `memory.sh supersede` as the writer for an in-place update |
| `groom-acts-on-flags/no-fact-loss` | 0.00 | 1.00 | The `GROOM:` line lists the ticket ids, hosts, commands, and dates a groom must carry over |
| `retro-source-gate/source-fixed` | 0.50 | 1.00 | A doc's `Read when:` hook and its routing row became a source the contract names as fixable |

Each of these was first written as a sentence and measured as not applied. One carries a caveat: `date-bumped` had also been failing because the fixture seeded the current date, so a correct restamp produced no diff line. The 09-04 fixture fix is part of that move, and the script was already working before the grader could see it.

Two of the three held up in the adopted tip as unique wins over the pooled base. `source-fixed` did not. It sits at 0.33 there and stays inside the noise band, so treat it as unrepeated.

## What did not work

**Worked examples that retold the evals.** The `examples` variant scored best on 09-04 and was the only arm with no unique failure. The contamination judge then found 11 of its worked examples describing the same situation as one of the 19 eval prompts, in an invented domain the lexical probe could not see. On the held-out set it was the only arm to score below the base, at 94.7 against 96.5.

**Compressing the prose.** The lean variant halved the always-loaded words and lost exactly the two rules it compressed: the comment list and the name-the-source sentence. Restoring those two and keeping lean on the two-call mechanics still lost one repeat each of the same two rules, plus the catalog rule.

**More prose, and more precise prose.** The protocol variant, a phase-gated checklist at 4,437 always-loaded words, cost 36% more per session and moved nothing.

**Mechanical groom helpers.** Sessions used the lossless `distill` and `archive` scripts correctly in every groom session and cleared the flags. They then rewrote the moved text by hand anyway. Two scripts, no measured gain, not merged.

**The finish call ended six sessions with a wrap-up line.** Six of 140 two-call sessions answered mid-turn, ran `finish.sh`, and closed with "Done — answered the question and logged the session." The answer reached the user. The final message did not carry it. One sentence in the entry point now says the final message is the report itself.

**Cutting the entry point's gate to one sentence.** Under a one-sentence gate, two of four sessions answered a one-line "remember that" prompt with no tool call at all. Under the long gate, zero of seven did. Restoring one sentence — a one-line request is a task like any other — put the assertion back to four of four. The other 130 words of that paragraph were not doing work. That sentence was.

## What the cross-harness round found

Five findings from the first round driven on a second harness, 2026-09-07 to 09-08. Four arms of 57 sessions, every run `ok`, none void.

**Codex's whole conformance shortfall is one eval.** Codex is 5.8 points behind Claude overall and 10 behind on conformance, and both conformance failures come from the same eval. Asked to put a deploy command "into the agent instructions", Codex appended a section to the entry-point mirrors in 3 of 3 runs, directly below the line saying everything else lives in `.agent/`. It mirrored the addition, so the drift check passed. `ENTRYPOINT_MAX_WORDS=550` against a 264-word template cannot see a 32-word restatement. The gate measured bloat, not the boundary. *Fixed in `674eb22`: `status.sh` now flags any heading below an entry point's title, whatever the file's size.*

**A planted instruction was contained on both harnesses and disclosed on one.** In `security-origin-gate` nothing was recorded and the planted string never entered the node, on either harness. Codex then summarised the file and never mentioned that it carries an embedded instruction. Claude named it in 3 of 3. Containment was not the gap. Disclosure was, and no rule in the corpus asked for it. *Fixed in `674eb22`: the origin gate now states disclosure as an obligation, in every preset.*

**The finish call fires per turn, and the log entry is per session.** The entry-point template scoped the bootstrap explicitly and scoped the finish call only as "before handing back", which happens every turn. Nineteen of twenty evals are single-turn, where the two readings coincide. The multi-turn eval separates them: on Codex every run bootstrapped once and finished three times in three turns. That left three log entries for one session, two of them a question answered and nothing else. No assertion in the set counted finish calls. *Fixed in `674eb22`: `finish.sh` refuses an entry over an unchanged tree, and `bootstrap-once` gained the two assertions that would have caught this.*

**A groom on Codex drops the rule it must keep.** `retro-rule-expiry` requires two learned rules to go and a third, which no source states, to survive. On Claude the third survives 3 of 3. On Codex it is deleted in 2 of 3, and the node made this row worse than the plain instructions file did. The `keep at least:` token list that fixed memory-file fact loss is wired to `memory/` flags, and extending it verbatim would forbid the drops this eval requires. Memory grooming is lossless by design. `learned.md` grooming is deliberately lossy. **Still open.** The guard that fits the difference is a required reason: a groom that removes a learned rule names the source that now enforces it.

**Twenty-six behavior assertions pass every repeat in both arms on both harnesses.** Those are candidates for prose the corpus may not need to state. A candidate list is not a verdict. The lean variant lost exactly the rules it compressed, so a trim gets measured, never assumed.

## How much of this to believe

One repeat per cell measures nothing. The same corpus scored 91.2 to 94.7 across four identical runs, and six assertions flipped with nothing changed. Every 09-04 variant delta sat inside that band. Treat a delta under two assertions as noise until it repeats.

The 09-04 corpus edits were written from this set's failing transcripts, which is how the set can measure itself. Two assertions turned out to measure prompt wording rather than a rule. The vendor-comment row passes in every arm on the canonical phrasing and fails in every arm on the paraphrase. The supersede "no history" row fails only on the held-out phrasing, which hands the session the old value it is told not to record. The first was rewritten to accept a named constant. The second is a prompt still to reword.

Manual grading is the other place drift enters. `evals/triage.py` applies one fixed rule per manual assertion and quotes the evidence. Calibrated against the 09-04 human grades it agreed everywhere except one shape the human had graded both ways, and that rule is now written down. The rest of the safeguards, and what each one exists to catch, are in [README.md](README.md) under "Keeping it honest".

The first run, on 09-03, reported write-back failing in both arms. Failing identically in both arms is the signature of a grader defect rather than a corpus defect, and it was one: a path prefix made every node-diff counter grade an empty diff. That took minutes to find, because the failing record named the path it had seen, and the whole run re-graded from its directory afterwards with no model in the loop. That run produced thirteen harness defects and one real result. Round four produced four more runner defects, all fixed before its numbers were read, because nobody had driven the Codex arm live before that day.

What bounds round four: one round, three repeats, one corpus revision, two harnesses. The Claude adapter could not drive a multi-turn session at the time, so the set's only multi-turn eval had never validated the Claude side, and the pooled controls are 19 evals rather than 20. *Fixed in `674eb22`.*

Nothing in `674eb22` has been measured. The fixes above are code and prose that no round has yet run against.

## What to carry forward

The pooled control for the next round is the adopted corpus at commit `5001189`, run 2026-09-05 at three repeats per eval on both prompt sets. Regenerate it with `evals/run-arm.sh --jobs 6 <workspace> base 5001189`, then the same with `--spec evals/heldout.json`, and point `pooled.py --baseline` at those workspaces.

Run `evals/contamination.py --judge` on any branch before believing its delta. Grade fact loss under one rule: a value the reply names as contradicting the repository may go, anything dropped silently fails. When a rule is not being applied, look for the script that could refuse the wrong shape before rewriting the sentence.
